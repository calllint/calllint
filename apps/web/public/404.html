HTTP 404 · no published assessment

We publish no assessment at this address.

This is a statement about our records, not about a server or its publisher. It does not mean a server was withdrawn, removed, or found wanting. The most common cause is the most boring one: a name that was mistyped, or a subject we have never assessed.

A trust surface that answered 200 here would be making a claim it cannot support — and until recently this one did, returning the homepage for every unmatched address. An absent subject and a typo were indistinguishable on the wire. This page is the correction.

What this page does not tell you

Deliberately, nothing about the subject you asked for. We do not state that it was de-listed, and we do not serve a tombstone. A name can leave a published cohort for at least four reasons with opposite meanings — the publisher withdrew it, it moved past the published ceiling, our own projection dropped it, or we never looked — and the serving plane cannot tell which. Asserting one would be a claim about a third party that we have not measured. Heuristic decision support, never a verdict on someone by omission.

Finding what you were looking for

  • Search published Trust Pages by name — exact or prefix.
  • Browse the Trust sitemap for every published address.
  • Scanning something not published here? The CLI runs locally and offline: npx calllint scan --auto. It never executes the server it judges.

If you are a machine client

Request the JSON API rather than a page, and an absence answers as a document instead of HTML: 

GET /v1/public/resources/mcp-registry/<name>
→ 404 application/json
{ "schema": "calllint.partner-api.error.v0", "code": "not_found", … }

A .json address under /trust/ answers the same way. Both are a plain 404, never 410: gone is a claim about permanence we have no evidence for.

What our verdicts mean, when we do publish one

A published page carries one of four dispositions, and the boundaries matter more than the labels. SAFE means No blockers observed under the evidence we had — it is Not a proof of runtime safety. UNKNOWN is never SAFE: it records that we could not verify, which is a different thing from a clean result.
